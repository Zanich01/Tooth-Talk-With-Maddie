Understand your smile

A healthier smile. A healthier you.

Oral health is part of whole-person care. Here is how to understand the connections without the alarm.

The key takeaway

A link between two conditions does not prove that one causes the other.

What you can do today

Update your medication list and share any health changes with your dental team.

01

What the research tells us

Researchers study links between gum disease and conditions including heart disease and pregnancy complications. Shared risk factors and inflammation may help explain these associations.

02

Diabetes and your mouth

Diabetes can affect the immune system and slow healing. Poorly controlled diabetes is associated with more frequent and severe periodontal disease, and periodontal disease can make blood sugar harder to manage. Improving control of one may help with managing the other. Share your diabetes history with your dental team and continue the care plan from your medical team.

03

Keep your care team connected

Bring an up-to-date medication list and tell your dental team about health changes, pregnancy, and ongoing treatment. Ask how these may affect your oral care.

04

Medications and dry mouth

Some medications can contribute to dry mouth, which raises the risk of decay and discomfort. Tell your dental and medical teams about persistent dryness, and discuss medication concerns with the prescriber before changing anything. Compare dry mouth care formats.
