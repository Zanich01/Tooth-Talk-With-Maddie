Get to know your gums

A simple guide to gum inflammation, periodontal disease, and the care that comes next.

The key takeaway

Bleeding gums deserve attention. A professional exam can tell you what is happening.

What you can do today

Notice changes in your gums? Book an assessment rather than trying to diagnose the stage yourself.

01

Notice the early signs

Gum inflammation, swelling, tenderness, bleeding, persistent bad breath, or gums pulling away from teeth can signal a problem. Plaque can harden into tartar, which requires professional removal.

02

Gingivitis: inflammation at the gumline

Early gum disease can often improve with consistent brushing, cleaning between teeth, and professional care. Ask your dental team to demonstrate a technique that works for you.

03

Periodontitis: deeper support is affected

Periodontitis affects the tissues and bone that support teeth. Scaling and root planing, often called a deep cleaning, removes deposits below the gumline and cleans affected root surfaces. Treatment and ongoing follow-up aim to control disease; home care remains important.

04

What your dental team checks

An examination may include gum-pocket measurements, your medical history, and X-rays to assess bone support. A single measurement alone is not a complete diagnosis. Loose teeth or pain when chewing should be assessed.
