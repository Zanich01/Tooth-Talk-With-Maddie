Cavity prevention & decay

Understand what happens beneath the surface, and give your everyday routine a clear purpose.

The key takeaway

Early mineral loss can sometimes be reversed. An established cavity needs dental treatment.

What you can do today

Make your technique count: see brushing and between-teeth guides.

01

How decay starts

Bacteria use sugars and starches to produce acids that remove minerals from enamel. Repeated exposure can lead to a cavity. Early decay may have no symptoms.

02

Early changes vs. a cavity

A white spot can signal early mineral loss. Saliva and fluoride can help repair enamel before a hole forms. Once a cavity develops, a dentist usually restores the damaged tooth with a filling.

03

Your everyday foundations

Brushing matters, but cavity risk also depends on frequent sugar exposure, saliva, fluoride, and other individual factors. Fluoride helps enamel resist mineral loss; sealants protect grooves on chewing surfaces. Brush with fluoride toothpaste, clean between teeth, and ask which additional prevention fits your risk.

04

When to book a visit

Pain or sensitivity deserves an assessment; it does not by itself confirm a cavity. Treatment depends on how much of the tooth is affected: a filling may repair a cavity, while deeper damage may require other treatment. For swelling, significant pain, or an injury, contact your dentist promptly. Read the ADA emergency guide.
